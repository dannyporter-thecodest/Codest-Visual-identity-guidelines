# 03. Writing register

Formal, precise and professional. Write as a senior consultancy reporting to a client's board, not as a blog or a sales page.

## Improve the writing, keep the terms

The job is to make the source better, not to re-create it. Rewrite sentences for clarity, structure and register, but keep the source's own terms for everything factual:

- **What was built:** keep the source's name for it. Source "custom logistics platform" stays "custom logistics platform", not "real-time supply chain platform".
- **Features and capabilities:** keep the source's wording. "Predictive analytics for inventory management" stays as is, not "AI demand forecasting".
- **Technologies, systems and integrations:** exactly as named on the source (ERP, WMS, Bluetooth Smart).
- **The client:** use the source's description. "A major logistics company operating across multiple regions" is not reworded into a new descriptor with new claims.
- **Processes and outcomes:** "order processing and route planning" stays "order processing and route planning".

Allowed: fixing grammar and spelling (British English), splitting or joining sentences, removing filler and waffle ("powerful", "significantly"), and choosing which source term to use where the source uses more than one for the same thing. Never shorten a source term ("sensor-based technology" is not "sensors").

Not allowed: a new name, a new category ("platform" for an "application"), a broader or narrower claim than the source makes, or a term from another case study.

| Source | Re-created (wrong) | Improved (right) |
|---|---|---|
| a custom logistics platform | one real-time supply chain platform | a custom logistics platform |
| a centralized hub for tracking shipments | one central control tower | a centralised hub for tracking shipments |
| predictive analytics features enabled the client to forecast inventory needs | AI-driven demand forecasting | predictive analytics enabled the client to forecast inventory needs |

## Rules

- Full, complete sentences. No casual idioms, dramatic phrasing or rhetorical lines.
- Precise verbs: delivered, engineered, implemented, integrated, enabled, required, maintained.
- "The Codest" in the third person in the hook, impact statement and Project description. "We" is allowed in Approach.
- Facts stated plainly. If a sentence would need a fact the source does not have, leave the sentence out.
- British English (organisation, programme, optimise).
- No em dashes or en dashes. Use a comma, a colon or a new sentence.
- No exclamation marks.
- No vague intensifiers: significantly, greatly, hugely, truly, incredibly.
- No marketing filler: seamless, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, game-changer, leverage, empower, top-notch, world-class, robust (unless technical and specific).

## Too casual, and the professional version

These were flagged in review. Use the right-hand column as the standard.

| Too casual | Professional |
|---|---|
| A safety platform that arrived after the peak would have arrived too late to protect anyone. | Any delay in delivery would have reduced the application's value to organisations complying with social distancing guidelines during the pandemic. |
| ...built and running while the need was urgent. | ...delivered within the timeframe of the pandemic response. |
| Sensitive data: contact and health information needed strong protection from day one. | Data security: the application processed health-related data, requiring strong encryption, strict access controls and ongoing regulatory compliance. |
| We built both the frontend and the backend. | The Codest was responsible for both frontend and backend development. |
| The backend was built for scale: it takes in sensor data as it arrives... | The backend was engineered for scalability, supporting high volumes of real-time sensor data while maintaining consistent performance. |
| ...a workplace safety platform in about 3 months | ...turned its sensor-based technology into a space management application (timeline goes in Results, from the source) |
| ...with The Codest owning the software | (The Codest is not named in the title) |

## Key challenge bullets

Pattern: `Label: one sentence stating the requirement and why it mattered.`

- Good: "Real-time monitoring: the system had to track interactions and distances between users in real time across large office spaces."
- Too casual: "Real time: it had to work live, all the time."
