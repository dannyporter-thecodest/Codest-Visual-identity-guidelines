# 02. Titles

**The title names what was built or changed.** That is what every TSH title has in common: the client (or a descriptor) as the subject, a plain verb, and a concrete system or product. Readers want to see the "what" first.

Statistics never lead the title. They go in Results and the stats band (`04-statistics.md`). A number may appear in the title only to describe the scale of the thing that was built ("across 63+ countries", "for 13 million travellers"), and it must also appear in Results.

## Shape: company, technology, outcome. Nothing else.

`[Company or descriptor] + [technology, in the source's words] + [outcome, in the source's words]`

| Company | Technology | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Estimote | turned its sensor-based technology | into a space management application |
| A major logistics company | built a custom logistics platform | to automate manual processes |

No context, no second outcome, no features. "During the COVID-19 pandemic" and "for social distancing" are true, but they belong in the hook and Challenge, not the title.

## Keep the terminology, cut the waffle

Simple and to the point, but every term stays exactly as the source writes it.

- **Never shorten or reword a source term.** "sensor-based technology" stays "sensor-based technology", not "sensors". "space management application" stays whole, not "app" or "application". "COVID-19 pandemic" is not shortened to "COVID-19".
- **The main deliverable.** The outcome is the ultimate thing the source says was delivered, not a secondary feature. Estimote's source built a "space management application"; contact tracing was one feature of it, so the title says "space management application". Secondary features go in Project description and Results.
- **Cut waffle words, not terms.** Waffle is anything that is not the company, the technology or the outcome:
  - context and timing phrases ("during the COVID-19 pandemic", "at the height of")
  - a second purpose or outcome ("for social distancing", "and improved efficiency")
  - feature lists ("with real-time tracking, route optimisation and predictive analytics")
  - filler and intensifiers ("successfully", "significantly", "innovative", "cutting-edge")
  - weak joins ("in order to", "which", "that", "helped to")
- **8 to 14 words, 3 lines at most** at desktop width (44 characters a line). *(build)* If a title is still too long after cutting the waffle, the outcome is too broad: pick the main deliverable.

| Wordy | Right |
|---|---|
| Estimote turned its sensor-based technology into a space management application for social distancing during the COVID-19 pandemic (17 words, 4 lines) | Estimote turned its sensor-based technology into a space management application (10 words, 2 lines) |
| A major logistics company built a custom logistics platform to automate order processing and route planning (16 words, 3 lines) | A major logistics company built a custom logistics platform to automate manual processes (13 words, 3 lines) |

| Wrong: terminology changed | Why |
|---|---|
| Estimote turned its sensor-based technology into a space management application | "sensors" shortens "sensor-based technology"; "COVID-19" shortens the source's phrase; the context is waffle |
| Estimote turned its sensors into a contact tracing system during COVID-19 | names a secondary feature instead of the main deliverable |

## Formulas

| # | Use when | Formula | TSH example | Codest pattern |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | An existing system became something new | [Client] turned [old thing] into [new thing] | Reservix turned its CMS into a sales engine with AI suggestions and third-party automations | Estimote turned its sensor-based technology into a space management application |
| 2 | A new product or platform was built | [Client] built [system] for [purpose] | Bakkt built Zaira for agentic payments across 63+ countries with anti-hallucination payment guardrails | [Client] built [platform] for [purpose] |
| 3 | A manual or legacy process was replaced | [Client] built/replaced [system] to automate [process] | (closest: Mr & Mrs Smith modernized its hotel platform and integrated it with its new parent) | A major logistics company built a custom logistics platform to automate manual processes |
| 4 | A legacy platform was modernised | [Client] modernised [platform] [and what else] | A bank modernized its legacy platform for 30,000 customers without a single disruption | [Client] modernised its [platform] and [result in words] |
| 5 | Team extension or a placed specialist | [Client] [scaled/extended] its [team] to [deliver what] | A banking tech provider quickly scales the e-Wallet team to deliver high-complexity services in parallel | [Client] extended its [product] team with senior [technology] developers to [deliver what] |
| 6 | Unnamed client | Any formula above, with a factual descriptor as the subject | One FinTech gained a competitive edge with an MCP-based AI chatbot | A Nordic payments company replaced its legacy ledger with [system] |

## Check every title

- Follows company, technology, outcome, in that order, and nothing else.
- Every term is exactly as the source writes it. Only waffle words are cut.
- Names the system, product or team that was built, changed or extended.
- Uses the source's own name for the system where it has one ("a custom logistics platform", "an IoT office management platform"). Do not invent a new description of it.
- Client first. The Codest is not named.
- 8 to 14 words, and 3 lines or fewer at desktop width. Reads in one breath. *(build)*
- Does not start with a number or an outcome verb (reduced, increased, boosted, cut, saved, improved, enabled, delivered). *(build)*
- No statistic as the point of the title. A number only as the scale of the "what", and it also appears in Results. *(build)*
- Past tense verb: turned, built, automated, replaced, modernised, extended, launched.
- No hype ("revolutionised", "game-changing") and no casual hedges ("in about", "around", "roughly").
